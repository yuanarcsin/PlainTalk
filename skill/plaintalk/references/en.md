# English track · surface rules

> Read only when the output is English. Chinese and English fail differently. Chinese stacks concepts in one sentence; English hides the actor behind passives and noun stacks.

## Six failures

| Failure | Test | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Agentless passive | Who did it is missing and matters | Name the actor, or use an active verb |
| Noun stacking | Three or more abstract nouns in a row; technical names can stay | Break into a phrase with a verb |
| Tier-1 vocabulary | delve, leverage, seamless, robust, holistic, foster, myriad, pivotal | Prefer the concrete word; do not use these to sound important |
| Nominalisation | “perform an analysis of” / “the implementation of” | Use the verb: analyse, implement |
| Em-dash aside | A dash inserts a second thought | Comma, colon, or a new sentence |
| Hedge stack | may/might/could possibly | At most one hedge |

## Also cut

- Chatbot residue: “Great question”, “Let’s dive in”, “In today’s fast-paced world”.
- Marketing: “game-changing”, “best-in-class”, “excited to announce”.
- -ing tails that add analysis: “..., highlighting the importance of X”.
- False ranges: “from X to Y” when X and Y are not endpoints of one scale.
- Title Case in headings; use sentence case.

## Format

- One space after punctuation; no space before it.
- Numbers and units: 50,000 rows, 30 minutes, 2.4.0.
- Code, commands and paths in code spans or blocks.
- Headings in sentence case; no skipped levels.

## Preserve

Same as SKILL rules 1 and 7; only the English surface: numbers, conditions, hedges; attribution; mechanism claims; the source’s own metaphors when they carry the mechanism.

## One bottom line

The common failure is not too much decoration but too little specific information. Deleting filler is not enough. The facts come from the source, not from the model.
